Introduction

The terms “fundamentals” and “computing” are often used when discussing technology. Fundamentals refer to the basic ideas or main parts that support a subject, while computing involves processing information by following logical instructions to produce a result. When these two ideas are combined, Fundamentals of Computing can be understood as the basic concepts that explain how computers and other digital devices work. These concepts include both hardware and software.

Throughout the course, we covered several topics that helped us understand how computer systems and their electronic component’s function. Chapter 0, Introduction, explained terms such as algorithms, programs, software, and hardware. We also learned that algorithms are part of everyday life because we often follow a set of steps to complete a task. The chapter introduced the Euclidean algorithm, which is connected to the process of finding a greatest common divisor, as well as early calculating devices such as the abacus and gear-based mechanical calculators.

Chapter 1 focused on Data Storage. This topic was especially important to our project because the binary calculator is based on number systems and the representation of information using binary digits, or 1s and 0s. The chapter also covered Boolean logic, including AND, OR, XOR, and NOT operations, which are important in digital circuits. We also discussed computer hardware, including the CPU, memory, and input and output devices, along with different methods of storing data.

Other topics that contributed to our understanding of the project were Data Manipulation, Operating Systems, Networks, and the Internet. Data manipulation involves working with information and changing it through different operations. One important part of this topic was the architecture of a computer, especially the CPU. The Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU) performs mathematical operations, such as addition and subtraction, as well as logical operations such as AND, OR, and NOT. The Control Unit has a different role: it directs the flow of instructions and data within the computer. We also learned about machine language, including machine instructions, instruction sets, opcodes, and operands. Arithmetic and logical instructions allow a computer to produce new values from existing bit patterns. Examples include ADD, AND, OR, and XOR, which relate directly to the operations used in our calculator.

Operating systems and the Internet were also important parts of the course. We use computers and other devices regularly, but we do not always think about the processes happening in the background. Learning about operating systems helped us understand the difference between application software and the kernel, as well as why some applications work with certain operating systems and others do not. We also discussed how an operating system helps manage the computer and its resources. The Internet was useful while completing this project because we used online information, examples, and ideas to guide our work. We also learned about the World Wide Web and how websites are made up of connected pages. Hypertext and hyperlinks help users move between related information, and we applied these ideas in our document and website. In addition, the course introduced the graphical user interface (GUI) and the Human-Machine Interface. These concepts helped us consider how to make our calculator webpage easy to understand and use, including choosing icons and other features that clearly communicate their purpose.

Machine language is another concept that connects to our project. It is considered a first-generation programming language and is represented using binary. Although people usually write programs in higher-level languages, those instructions are eventually translated into forms that a computer can process. Our project also involved the use of programming languages such as C++ and Java in developing the binary calculator and its webpage.

The course also covered Computer Graphics and Artificial Intelligence. Computer graphics includes areas such as clipping, shading, reflection, and animation. These concepts can help make a webpage more attractive and easier to use. For our project, Mr. Zaheem, our graphics member took on the visual design and helped make the webpage look more organized and appealing. Artificial Intelligence was another useful topic during the project. It provided support when we needed ideas, examples, or guidance. However, completing the project still required our group to understand the material, make decisions, and apply what we had learned.

Overall, our group took the project as an opportunity to apply the knowledge we gained throughout the Fundamentals of Computing course. Developing the binary calculator required us to work together and use the information we learned rather than depend entirely on AI. The main topics connected to the project included the history of computers, Data Storage, Data Manipulation, Operating Systems, Networks and the Internet, Software Engineering, Computer Graphics, and Artificial Intelligence. Each topic contributed in some way to the development of the calculator and its webpage. As a group, we had to pay attention to these areas and make sure that our final project demonstrated what we had learned.
